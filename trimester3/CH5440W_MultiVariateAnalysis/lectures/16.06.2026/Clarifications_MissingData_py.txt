
1. Optimum Length of Window in Moving Mean

Better NOT to have a big window in moving mean method as averaging
across a wide horizon
a)  reduces variability
b)  But smoothens the noise
c)  Smoothening out may not be bad but increasing window by too much
    leads to over smoothening of the data,  destroying real signals and
    trends
d)  May lead to infeasible estimates in imputation of missing values

1. Optimum Length of Window in Moving Mean



   When we talk about "introducing irrelevant historical data" in the context of a moving average (or
   any rolling time-series method), we mean that the mathematical window has grown so large that it
   starts pulling in past data points that no longer reflect the current reality or trend of the system.
   A moving mean assumes that data points close to each other in time are similar. If you use a very
   wide window, you end up averaging data from completely different cyclical patterns, seasons, or
   structural states.

2. What is then Optimum Window Length in Moving Mean?

❖    Not too big as you may over smooth the data destroying real
    signals and trends and misses local variations
❖   Not too small as data may be sensitive to effects of noise
❖   Not possible to give a definitive window size and depends upon the
    context and
❖   whether data is rather static (larger window to reduce noise effects)
    or very dynamic (small window to capture fast changing local effects
    so that the mean predicted value does not lag behind)
