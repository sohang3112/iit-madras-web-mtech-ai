            What is Meant by Regression?


  A return to former state of existence


  Reduced symptoms    or lower severity of a disease instead of its

  progression (regression of disease)


  The relation between input and output is called regression because it

  refers to a statistical tendency of data points to move ("regress" or

  "step back") towards the average (mean) value.

            What is Meant by Regression?


  Historical Origin: "Regression to the Mean"


  Galton's Observations (1870s-1880s): While studying the inheritance

  of traits, Galton observed that children of very tall parents were

  usually shorter than their parents but taller than the average, while

  children of very short parents were usually taller than their parents

  but shorter than the average.

              What is Meant by Regression?


   Regressing Toward Mediocrity: Galton called this phenomenon "regression

   toward mediocrity" or "regression to the mean," meaning that data points

   extreme in value tend to "regress" back toward the average value.


   The "Line of Regression": To visualize and model this, Galton used a straight

   line to summarize the relationship, which he termed the "regression line”


   Machine Learning Definition: In modern data science, regression refers to a

   supervised learning technique used to predict a continuous numerical output

   (e.g., price, temperature, sales volume) based on input features.

         The Regression Line of Galton


   Regression to the mean is an important statistical phenomenon which is

   often neglected and may lead to misleading conclusions.


   For example, statistics on the impact of CCTVs indicated that they reduced

   crime by around 100 robberies


   This inference was based on the reduction in crime after the CCTVs had

   been installed.


   But, CCTVs are often installed after there have been an unusually high

   number of robberies


   So you may generally anticipate that the number of robberies return to

   normal levels afterwards anyway

            Regression and Causality


  Let there be a strong statistical relation between the response

  variable Y and the explanatory or predictor variable X


  But it does not imply in any way that Y depends causally on X i.e.,

  X was solely responsible for causing Y


  Irrespective of the strong statistical relation between X and Y, do

  not attribute a cause-and-effect pattern to the regression model.
